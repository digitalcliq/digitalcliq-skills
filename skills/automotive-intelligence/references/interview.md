# The GM Interview

8 categories, 3 rounds. Present each round as one chat message: intro line, then the categories with their bullets. Bullets are inspiration to riff on, not required fields. Always remind the user they can dictate (phone or Mac dictation, then paste), drop links, or point at files.

Opening framing, send before Round 1 (adapt lightly, keep the spirit):

> This is a brain dump, not a quiz. Three rounds, eight topics total. For each topic: talk to me like you'd talk to a new GSM you actually trust. Dictate on your phone and paste it, type it, drop me links or files, whatever is fastest. Skip anything. The more real you are here, the less I'll ever have to ask you again.

---

## Round 1: Your store and you

### 1. You and your store
- Your name, and the store: dealership name, brand(s) on the sign, city and market
- Your title and how long you've run this store; how long you've been in the car business
- Ownership structure: dealer principal, family store, group-owned? Who do you answer to, and how involved are they day to day?
- Store size: roughly how many employees, how many salespeople, service bays
- What this store is known for in the market, good or bad

### 2. The numbers that run your month
- New and used volume targets, and where you actually land in a typical month
- Front and back gross expectations; where the money is really made at your store
- Service absorption, roughly; how much fixed ops carries the store
- The numbers you personally check every single day, in order
- Where you are against forecast right now, and what's driving that

### 3. Management style and communication
This one matters most. Everything the assistant does gets tuned to it.
- How do you run the store: hands-on on the floor, or through your managers? Numbers-first or people-first?
- How do you want information delivered: bullet points or narrative? Lead with the answer or walk the logic? How much detail before you're annoyed?
- Your hot buttons: the things that set you off when you see them (unworked leads, dirty lot, late managers, excuses without a plan, whatever they are)
- Your non-negotiables: the standards you never bend on
- When and how you work best: early bird desk time, late closer, weekends on the floor?
- How do you praise and how do you correct? Public or private?
- Pet phrases you actually use, and words or approaches that annoy you

---

## Round 2: How the store runs

### 4. Departments and how you structure them
For each department you have (New, Used, Finance, Service, Parts, Body Shop, BDC), riff on:
- Who runs it and how much rope you give them
- How YOU want that department structured and run: the meeting cadence, the process you insist on, what "running right" looks like to you
- Which one keeps you up at night right now, and which one runs itself
- Anything you're restructuring or wish you could

### 5. Your people
- Your key managers: GSM, sales managers, desk, F&I, service manager, parts manager, BDC lead. Names and roles
- Who's a star, who's developing, who you're worried about
- Who covers what when you're out
- Pay plan philosophy, if you want the assistant to understand it

### 6. Tech stack and vendors
- DMS (CDK, Reynolds, Tekion, Dealertrack, other)
- CRM (VinSolutions, Elead, DriveCentric, other), and honestly, how well it's actually worked
- Website provider, inventory/merchandising tools, pricing tools (vAuto etc.)
- Third-party listings: Cars.com, CarGurus, Autotrader, which ones you pay for
- Agencies and marketing vendors, roughly what you spend a month on advertising
- Co-op: are you using your full OEM co-op, and who handles claims
- The vendor you'd fire tomorrow if you had a replacement, and the one you'd never give up

---

## Round 3: Market and friction

### 7. Comp set, market, and OEM programs
- The 3 to 5 stores you actually lose deals to. Names, brands, roughly how far away
- What they do better than you, and what you do better than them
- Your market: growing, shrinking, price-driven, payment-driven?
- Your OEM programs: standards/facility programs, incentive cadence, allocation situation, how your zone rep relationship is
- Anything the factory does that helps or hurts you right now

### 8. Pain points and drains
Be specific here; this is where the assistant earns its keep.
- What work slows you down the most in a normal week?
- The reports or prep you build by hand over and over (manager meeting prep, forecast sheets, OEM submissions, whatever)
- The recurring fires: the thing that blows up every month like clockwork
- Decisions that have been sitting unmade because you haven't had time to think them through
- If you could hand three tasks to a sharp assistant tomorrow and never touch them again, what are they?
