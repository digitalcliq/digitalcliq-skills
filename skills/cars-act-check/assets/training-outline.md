# {DEALERSHIP NAME} CARS Act Training Program

**Generated per client. Insert that client's own recent audit findings as examples wherever {REAL EXAMPLE} appears; real findings train better than hypotheticals. Each role module: 30-45 minutes plus a 10-question quiz. Certification tracked by {ROLE}; annual refresh. Content follows Cal. Civ. Code § 1784.20 et seq. and the CNCDA CARS Act Compliance Guide v1.2 (2026-08-07) with its Webinar FAQ (2026-08-31); worked examples are adapted from CNCDA Appendices D, E, and F with fictitious figures.**

## Module 0: Everyone (15 min)

- What SB 766 is, why it exists, operative 2026-10-01 with no delay pending, enforcement through consumer complaints, the DMV, the Attorney General, district attorneys, and private UCL/CLRA suits; rights are non-waivable.
- CNCDA's four biggest risks in the first 120 days: installed add-ons left out of the total price, first-reply failures in the BDC and CRM, records that cannot be produced, and three-day return mechanics.
- The one-sentence version of each pillar: total price everywhere, the first written reply carries the price, add-ons are optional and some are banned, used vehicles $50k or less get three free days, everything is kept two years.
- The house rule that covers 80% of risk: if you put a vehicle and a number in writing, the total price rides with it, and it happens inside {CRM}, never on a personal phone. The safest first reply is the total price and nothing else.

## Module 1: Sales (30 min)

- Prohibited misrepresentations walkthrough (CC 1784.40), each of the 13 categories with a showroom example; the three CNCDA hot spots: availability at the advertised price, trade-in payoff promises, pre-approval language. {REAL EXAMPLE}
- The advertised price is a ceiling: any customer can buy at the online total price while the unit is unsold, whether or not they saw it, unless the offer expired. A verbal quote does not trigger the written rules; a worksheet or screen you show does, even if the customer does not keep it.
- Payment conversations: every written payment column carries the total the customer will pay and any assumed down payment or trade value; a comparison that highlights lower payments carries the lower-payment notice. The default: no payment figures until the deal is desked.
- Walk-ins with no prior contact: the first worksheet or quote is the compliance event; use the Total Price Disclosure form (LAWCA-TOTPRCDISC) or a CRM message; no signature needed, note a refusal.
- 3-day right to cancel scripts: the period runs from signing, not delivery, three calendar days ending at close of business, extended when day three is a closed day; 400 miles; used units $50,000 or less; the restocking fee comes out of the refund first and only a shortfall is due at the door; what you may never say or do (impede, demand the fee in cash, "the manager isn't here to process it").
- Trade-in and down payment handling when deals don't finalize; why trades are held during the window; why an over-allowed trade becomes a cash floor if the customer cancels after it is sold.
- Quiz scenarios: bait price on a sold unit, "everyone's approved", quoting from a personal cell, a customer who returns on day three at 5:30 pm on a day the store closes at 6, a $300 down payment against a $480 restocking fee.

## Module 2: F&I (45 min)

- Add-on disclosure duty: written optional-status disclosure at the first written presentation (menu, worksheet, DR screen), documented at signing on the OPD (LAWCA-OPD); § 1632 language matching with the figures on the translated form; the seven prohibited product types with real menu examples; eligibility screens (EV oil change, no-converter marking, void service contracts, GAP outside the § 2982.12 loan-to-value). {REAL EXAMPLE}
- Menu presentation: no pre-checked products, genuine election, plain-English descriptions (never "protection package"), benefit-based selling only, penetration rates as a structural flag, provider payment within 10 days.
- The Pre-Contract Disclosure (LAWCA-PCD 10/26) must match the contract line by line; regenerate it when the deal changes; the lower-payment notice is preprinted on every form and how to explain that.
- Pre-installed devices and hybrid products: the hardware is in the total price with a real base warranty, the upgrade is optional, never "free hardware, paid activation", never deactivate on decline; catalytic converter marking is a benefit on its own, bundled reimbursement is an insurance question.
- 3-day cancellation mechanics: separate statutory disclosure (LAWCA-CANCELDSCL) with the right deadline, verbatim first-page contract notice, cancellation and refund within 48 hours, two business days after verification for a check, unverified funds never a reason to refuse, trade-in payout as the greatest of allowance / gross resale / supported fair market value less indebtedness paid, itemized Acknowledgment (LAWCA-CANCELACKN), Failure Notice (LAWCA-CANCELFAIL) only on specific supported grounds with a manager's signature, DMV rollback paperwork and tax reversal.
- Document retention: unfinalized deal jackets count; every worksheet for a signing customer; where they get filed; wet-signed originals kept 90 days before scanning.
- Quiz scenarios: EV oil change on the menu, GAP on a near-LTV-cap deal, customer cancels day 2 with the trade already wholesaled for less than the allowance, a cash deal (no PCD, OPD stands alone), customer paid by personal check and wants the refund tomorrow.

## Module 3: BDC / Internet (30 min)

- First written communication rule deep dive: what counts (email, SMS, chat, DR message, worksheet shown to a walk-in), what triggers it (specific vehicle OR amount OR financing term), and CNCDA's rule that a reply to a vehicle-specific inquiry is a response about that vehicle even if it never names the car.
- The approved templates in {CRM}: where they live, why they can't be edited, Strategy 1 (price-forward merge) vs Strategy 2 (bare receipt, then the price on the first substantive reply) and which one this store uses; the receipt may not mention the vehicle, a price, marketing, or a credit application.
- SMS consent flows carry consent text only. AI chat states the total price on the next substantive reply after a unit is named, or sends only a transfer notice. Never a link in place of the price. Credit-only inquiries get no numbers until a vehicle is chosen.
- Chat and third-party leads: transcripts sync to CRM, no off-platform quoting, personal phones only through the CRM-routed app, no availability promises on units pending sale, brokers get the total price too.
- Date every priced template; the price you send binds the store to that customer.
- Quiz scenarios: customer texts "what's your best price on the white X5", writing back from a personal phone, chat bot quoted last month's price, an auto-reply that says "apply for credit now" to a lead on a specific stock number, a lead that asks "can I get approved" with no vehicle.

## Module 4: Marketing (30 min)

- Total price in every ad referencing a specific vehicle or amount: website, DR tool, Meta, Google, email, mailers, third-party listings, social, employee personal accounts; class ads ("starting at", no identified unit) are outside the trigger but need the quantity disclosure and VINs when four or fewer. {REAL EXAMPLE}
- Compliant ad anatomy (CNCDA Appendix D): model and model-year, VIN or license number (not stock number alone), "Total Price" as the prominent figure with installed items included, MSRP labeled and smaller if shown, Factory Rebate then Net Cost (never "price"), the Plus Disclosure verbatim, "Price does not apply to leases" where relevant, an expiration date; payment ads add the Reg Z set, lease ads the Reg M set.
- Dead practices: "Call for price" and price-gating buttons, MSRP-only or "MSRP is not the selling price", pay-or-remove and "excludes dealer-installed accessories", $0 optional-products addendums, "out-the-door" labels, dealer cash back and "up to $X in rebates".
- The federal doc-fee question: FTC staff want the DPC in the prominent price, California keeps it out; this store's election ({INCLUDE DPC | CNCDA DUAL PRESENTATION}) and how to render it.
- One total price per vehicle across every surface; scheduled third-party listing audits; written correction requests to OEM-controlled listings; 48-hour delisting; the ad archive DigitalCLIQ keeps and why regulators will ask for it.
- Claims that trigger CC 1784.40: guarantees, pre-approval, government styling, "free" tied to a vehicle purchase (Veh. Code 11713.1(h)), strikethrough pricing.
- Quiz scenarios: lease banner with payment only for a stock-numbered unit, boosted post on a sold unit, "guaranteed $5k for your trade" campaign, a salesperson's Instagram story with a price, a TrueCar member price lower than the site.
