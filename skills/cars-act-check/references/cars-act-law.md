## Status

- Signed 2025-10-06. **Operative 2026-10-01** (§ 1784.28). No delaying or amending legislation enacted or pending as of 2026-08-31 (CNCDA FAQ Q69).
- Codified at Cal. Civ. Code § 1784.20 et seq. Non-exclusive (§ 1784.22): Veh. Code § 11713.1 advertising rules, § 11713.16, 13 CCR § 255 et seq., Reg Z, Reg M, and FTC Act § 5 all still apply on top. Liberally construed for consumers (§ 1784.30).
- Applies to licensed California dealers (autobrokers included, § 1784.31(c)) in retail sales, leases, and financing. Excluded from "motor vehicle": wholesale, vehicles not required to be registered under the Vehicle Code, fleet sales (more than one vehicle in a single transaction for business use), commercial purchasers (five or more vehicles from **that dealer** per year for business use; the count is per dealer), and vehicles with GVWR of 10,000 lbs or more. A single light-duty vehicle sold to a small business is generally covered. Auction sales of used vehicles are exempt from § 1784.41 and the three-day right (§§ 1784.41(g), 1784.43(i)).
- Consumer protections cannot be waived (§ 1784.21). Do not accept vendor forms that purport to obtain waivers.
- Enforcement (CNCDA Guide Part 8): no express private right of action and no direct DMV authority, but expect UCL (B&P § 17200), False Advertising Law, and CLRA suits; the Attorney General and district attorneys; DMV Vehicle Code theories on overlapping advertising rules; and FTC Section 5. SB 478 hidden-fee law: the dealer safe harbor (Veh. Code § 11713.27) covers only the fees enumerated in § 11713.1(b); excluding anything else (a required dealer-added product) is outside it. Enforcement actions overwhelmingly begin with consumer complaints.

## § 1784.40 Prohibited misrepresentations (thirteen categories, letters (a) to (m))

Dealers may not misrepresent material information about: (a) costs or terms of purchasing, financing, or leasing; (b) any cost, limitation, benefit, or other aspect of an add-on; (c) whether the final contract is a lease or a sale (presenting multiple payment options before sale is expressly permitted); (d) availability of vehicles at a total price the dealer communicated (safe harbor if the ad is withdrawn within 48 hours of sale per Veh. Code § 11713(c)); (e) whether a consumer has been or will be preapproved or guaranteed for any product, service, or term; (f) information on or about the consumer's financing application; (g) whether the dealer will keep cash down payments or trade-ins, charge fees, or initiate legal process if the deal is not finalized; (h) whether and when the dealer will pay off a trade-in and what happens if it misses the Veh. Code § 11709.4 window; (i) the remedy available if the dealer fails to sell or lease at the total price; (j) affiliation with or endorsement by any government agency or military department; (k) whether or when a vehicle may be repossessed; (l) whether a vehicle can be moved outside California or the United States; (m) any of the Act's required disclosures. CNCDA numbers these 1 to 13 in the same order. Daily-operations hot spots per CNCDA: availability at the advertised price (pull sold units fast), trade-in payoff promises, preapproval language in marketing.

## § 1784.41 Mandatory disclosures (total price, first communication, payments, add-ons)

Every § 1784.41 disclosure must be clear and conspicuous; failing that standard is itself the violation.

**Total price** (§ 1784.31(j)): the total sale price excluding only the Veh. Code § 11713.1(e) items (taxes, registration, tire fee, certificate fees, finance charges, dealer document processing charge, electronic filing charge, emission testing charge); **including** any dealer markup and every item installed on the vehicle at the time of the ad or communication; **never** reduced by a rebate. Must be disclosed:
- (a)(1) In any advertisement that references a specific vehicle for sale.
- (a)(2) In any advertisement that represents a monetary amount or financing term for a specific vehicle. Per CNCDA FAQ Q19 this covers lease ads that identify a unit (stock number, VIN, photo of an identified unit); MSRP used only to identify a class does not trigger it.
- (a)(3) In the **first written communication** with a consumer that references a specific vehicle for sale, or any monetary amount or financing term for any vehicle; disclosed at least once in the dealer's first response regarding that vehicle ((a)(3)(A)); retained two years and provided on written request ((a)(3)(B)).
- (f) Existing Veh. Code §§ 11713.1 and 11713.16 are not modified: Plus Disclosure verbatim on every web page displaying a price (§ 11713.1(c)(2)), VIN or license identification (§ 11713.1(a), stock number alone insufficient), "starting at" quantity (§ 11713.1(i)), expiration dating and the ceiling rule (§ 11713.1(e)), supplemental sticker rules (§ 11713.1(q)), showroom price list (§ 11709.3).
- (g) Auction sales of used vehicles exempt.

CNCDA advertising positions (Guide Part 2, FAQ Section A): an "advertisement" is any statement the dealer intentionally communicates to the public to promote a sale (13 CCR § 255.00(b)), including dealer-fed third-party listings, dealer stickers, mailers, email blasts, and social posts, employee personal accounts included. MSRP-only and "MSRP is not the selling price" both fail; MSRP may appear only labeled and no more prominently than the total price. "Contact dealer for price" fails; a CTA in place of the price is the online "call for price". The advertised price is a ceiling for everyone while the unit is unsold unless the ad expired, so date every price ad and CRM template. One total price per vehicle across site, digital retailing tool, syndicated listings, and lot; a lower figure anywhere the dealer disseminates becomes the advertised price. No intent element, no bona fide error defense. Rebates: total price first, "Factory Rebate" as a specific amount, after-rebate figure labeled "net cost" never "price", no dealer cash back, no "up to $X", no conflicting stacks, eligibility adjacent. "Pay or remove" and "excludes dealer-installed accessories" are dead for an identified unit; the dealer need not remove or discount a refused installed item. Addendums list installed equipment only, every item in the total price, never a $0 optional-products addendum, new vehicles only. Markups are dealer price adjustments inside the total price, never optional.

**First written communication** (§ 1784.41(a)(3); CNCDA FAQ Section B): the trigger is content and context, not channel. A reply to a vehicle-specific inquiry is a response regarding that vehicle whether or not it names the vehicle; a generic acknowledgment or credit invitation does not defer the duty. Compliant auto-reply configurations: merge the total price from inventory, or send a bare administrative receipt with no vehicle reference, marketing, or credit invitation and put the price in the first substantive reply. Text opt-in messages carry consent language only. After an AI agent hears a specific vehicle, the next substantive reply states the total price (a bare transfer notice may precede it). State the price in the message body; a link is supplemental and a retained link-only message will not show what was disclosed. A salesperson's message through the digital retailing tool counts if it shows the price and the tool retains it two years. Credit-only inquiries with no vehicle trigger nothing; avoid quoting figures until a unit is identified. Verbal quotes do not trigger; a worksheet, screen, or printout shown to a walk-in does, and the Reynolds Total Price Disclosure form (LAWCA-TOTPRCDISC) documents it. No signature required; note a refusal and keep the unsigned copy. Personal phones only through a CRM-routed dealership app (Labor Code § 2802 reimbursement applies). Each shopper gets the disclosure. An itemized first offer must show the total price as its own figure equal to the advertised price, add-ons separately with the optionality statement, and any payment with total of payments and consideration; the simplest compliant reply states the total price and nothing more. Brokers are dealers; comply as if the broker message were to the consumer. Out-of-state shoppers get the total price as a matter of course. Store first communications in the CRM by customer and stock number, exportable, and export the full two-year history before any CRM migration.

**Negotiation-stage disclosures** (§ 1784.41(b), (c), (d), (e); CNCDA Guide Part 3, FAQ Section C), each triggered by a written representation during negotiation, on financed, leased, and cash deals alike:
- (b) Any written add-on representation: disclose at least once, clearly and conspicuously, in writing, that the add-on is not required and the vehicle can be bought or leased without it. Trigger is the F&I menu, desking worksheet, or digital retailing screen, so disclose at first written presentation and document at signing (Reynolds LAWCA-OPD; the LAWCA-PCD 10/26 covers the five ASFA categories on financed deals and must match the contract exactly). Customer-ordered accessories installed after sale are due-bill add-ons requiring this disclosure and do not change the advertised total price. Plain descriptions, never "protection package".
- (c)(1) Any written monthly payment: disclose the total the consumer will pay at that payment after all scheduled payments; (c)(2) if that total assumes cash down or trade value, disclose the consideration (CNCDA best practice: applied rebates too). Every pencil column, lease columns as total of scheduled lease payments.
- (d) Any written comparison discussing lower payments: disclose that lower monthly payments often increase the total amount paid. A consumer's own use of an internet payment tool is not a violation; CNCDA reads that as plainly covering this notice and arguably the (c) total, so configure tools to show both anyway; dealer-sent messages through the tool carry full disclosures.
- Translation: (b) and (c)(2) and the cancellation disclosure in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, or Korean when the negotiation is primarily in that language (Civ. Code § 1632(b)); the translated form carries the figures; provide and retain both. No signature required for any of these; they are disclosures, not agreements, so the single-document rule (§ 2981.9) is not implicated.

## § 1784.42 Add-on products: benefit requirement and vendor payment

- (a) No charge for an add-on the buyer or lessee would not benefit from. Named no-benefit products: nitrogen tire products under 95% purity; products providing no coverage for the vehicle, consumer, or transaction; GAP not compliant with Civ. Code §§ 2982, 2982.2, 2982.12 (calculate and retain loan-to-value on every GAP deal); service contracts void for preexisting conditions; oil changes for EVs; catalytic converter marking on a vehicle without a converter (marking itself is a benefit under Veh. Code § 24020; a bundled theft-reimbursement benefit may be insurance under Ins. Code § 116); surface protection that voids the paint warranty. Build eligibility screens so these cannot be sold.
- (b) Pay the add-on provider within 10 days of signing unless a written agreement allows later payment and coverage is unaffected; proof of timely payment is a required record.
- (c) Safe harbor for products the customer selected and would benefit from, even if never used; requires genuine election, so no defaulted or pre-selected products. Very high penetration on an "optional" product is treated as evidence it was mandatory.
- CNCDA product groups (Guide Part 4): preinstalled equipment (in the price); hybrid products (physical component with a real but modest base warranty in the price, upgraded coverage optional in F&I); traditional F&I products (optional, disclosed, screened); due-bill accessories (not in the price). A device deactivated when the customer declines delivers no benefit on its own terms. Hardware is not "free" when its cost is recovered through a subscription. Keep a benefit file per product.

## § 1784.43 Three-day right to cancel (used vehicles $50,000 or less)

- Applies to retail sales and leases of used vehicles (Veh. Code § 665) at a purchase price or capitalized cost of $50,000 or less ($50,000 covered, $50,001 not). Free of charge, never a line item. Not new vehicles at any price, not motorcycles (§ 1784.31(k)(2)), not lease-end buyouts to the lessee in possession ((h); a market repurchase through auction likely does not qualify), not Vehicle Code auction sales ((i)). CNCDA: grant the right whenever the vehicle price is $50,000 or less even if add-ons push the amount financed higher.
- Clock (§ 1784.31(i)): three calendar days commencing the day after **execution** (not delivery), ending at the posted close of business, extended to the next open day if day three falls on a closed day (holidays included). 400-mile cutoff measured from execution ((a)(1)(B)). Execute at delivery where possible; a voluntarily longer deadline entered on the disclosure binds the dealer; a shorter one is a violation.
- Restocking fee (§ 1784.31(g)): 1.5% of sale price, floor $200, cap $600 (or actual shipping cost up to that cap where a shipping fee was charged), plus $1 per mile over 250 capped at $150. Deducted from the refund first; only a shortfall is due at the door ((a)(2)(B)). Demanding cash for a fee the refund covers is overcharging and impeding.
- Documents: separate "3-Day Right to Cancel Used Car Purchase or Lease" disclosure with the nine statutory elements ((b); Reynolds LAWCA-CANCELDSCL), first-page contract notice on every purchase and lease agreement, new and used ((e); Civ. Code §§ 2982(r), 2985.8(e); Reynolds RISC 553CA1026), 36-point physical showroom notice in each sales office and cubicle where written terms are discussed and each contract room (Veh. Code § 11709.2; motorcycle/OHV supplement where applicable). The repealed two-day contract cancellation option form (Veh. Code § 11713.21) may not be used after 2026-10-01 even by a store already giving free returns; a 72-hour purchased option or a $40,000 threshold describes repealed law.
- On exercise: cancel the contract and refund within 48 hours ((d)(1)); for non-verified payment methods, two business days after verification with documentation ((d)(2)); unverified funds never justify rejecting the cancellation. Return the trade-in if unsold; if sold or in title transfer, pay the greatest of contract allowance, gross resale price (reconditioning not deducted), or fair market value (presumed by a 7-day written offer the dealer honored or a nationally recognized pricing-guide printout), less only indebtedness actually paid, with an itemized receipt and redacted proof of the trade sale ((a)(3), (b)(9)). A return is a DMV rollback, not an unwind: DMV fees stay paid, refund the tax attributable to refunded amounts (Rev. & Tax. Code § 6012.3), registration fees are not a deduction, notify the lender.
- Prohibited ((c)): impeding exercise, overcharging the fee, withholding the down payment or trade-in, failing to refund timely, failing to refund the trade value when sold, failing to provide the trade-sale receipt, claiming damage without reasonable basis (the dealer bears that burden; photograph, and get a service diagnosis before a Failure Notice), and claiming the authorized person is unavailable. A defect that manifested after delivery and was not caused by the customer does not defeat the right.
- (j) Dealers may offer more (longer window, more vehicles, lower fee) and must then describe it in the disclosure and signage and apply it consistently; post-period returns are voluntary and uncapped.

## § 1784.44 Recordkeeping (two years from creation; failure to keep is itself a violation)

Keep: (a)(1) records showing that communications and advertisements of total price complied, including internet listings the dealer disseminated (excluding records generated solely by consumer-adjusted financing tools); (a)(2) all purchase orders and signed financing and lease documents, approved or not, plus all written sales, financing, and leasing communications with any customer who signs; (a)(3) add-on benefit records including service contracts, GAP agreements, proof of timely vendor payment, and GAP loan-to-value calculations; (a)(4) cancellation requests, refund proof, trade-in return proof; (a)(5) written complaints about sales, financing, leasing, or cancellations, written add-on inquiries, and written inquiries and responses about advertised vehicles (surveys, reviews, and testimonials excluded). Any legible form (§ 1784.44(b)). Separately, § 1784.41(a)(3)(B) requires the first written communication for two years, produced on written request, whether or not a deal happened.

CNCDA practical retention (Guide Part 7, FAQ Section F): the Act does not require archiving every ad; retain the DMS or inventory-provider feed export by vehicle and date and get the vendor's written confirmation it can produce two years of history; dated site and third-party captures supplement it. Keep creative with run dates and placement only where it states a total price or payment for a specific vehicle; mailers with mailing date and list identifiers. Worksheets: every one for signers; first communication plus inquiries and responses for non-buyers. Export the full CRM history before a migration. The two-year period sits alongside longer DMV and ASFA (seven years or contract length) periods; follow the longest. CNCDA advises against over-retention. Vendors do not carry the obligation; confirm readiness in writing and notify the manufacturer in writing about any preselected vendor that cannot comply (Veh. Code § 11713.13(f)(1)(E) indemnity).

## Federal note (changing area)

The FTC CARS Rule was vacated (5th Cir., 2025-01-27) and withdrawn; FTC Section 5 enforcement continues and has no grace period. In March 2026 the FTC sent warning letters to 97 dealer groups. In **September 2026 FTC staff issued the written Automobile Industry Pricing Transparency FAQs** (fourteen questions, staff views): the advertised price is the price any consumer can pay, excluding only charges a government requires the consumer to pay directly (Q2); the full doc fee goes inside the most prominent price at the highest mandatory amount, $40,000 plus $85 advertises as $40,085 (Q6), and state doc-fee rules are additive (Q7); every page stating any amount, SRPs included, shows the actual price as the most prominent figure by size and placement (Q4, Q5); conditional and finance-tied discounts stay outside it (Q5, Q9); lease due-at-signing includes upfront fees (Q8); not-on-lot units say so (Q10); stock photos only where truly representative, never on used units (Q11); dealer, vendor, and OEM share responsibility (Q12); calls and texts quoting a price are ads (Q3). On **2026-09-30 FTC staff (Michael Dingman, Bureau of Consumer Protection, via ComplyAuto 2026-10-05)** added: the fee test turns on who is legally obligated, so a state cap or authorization does not make a fee governmental and the electronic filing and emission testing charges go in the price when the dealer requires them; "in transit" means already shipped, so unbuilt units get their real status; a price CTA may sit beside the price but may not imply a lower one; savings claims may not read as inventory-wide; AI-generated ads carry the same duty. California keeps the DPC, electronic filing charge, and emission testing charge outside total price, so the two regimes pull apart on those three lines. CNCDA's dual-compliant format: "Total Price", "Document processing charge (not a governmental fee)", "Price including document processing charge", with the inclusive figure the most prominent amount (the FAQs ask for most prominent, not merely equal), taxes and government fees still excluded, never labeled "out-the-door". Full digest, question by question, in `Resources/automotive-guidelines/ftc-advertising-compliance.md`; the overlay is scanned as points 111 to 120 (section J of `references/inspection-points.md`).
